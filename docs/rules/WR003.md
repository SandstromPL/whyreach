# WR003: the service listens on a different port

- **Layer:** 5, listener
- **Confidence:** high when it listens on exactly one other port; medium with more (with more than three, the fix names no port); one level lower while its health is still `starting`
- **Supersedes:** WR001
- **Since:** v0.1 (M2)

## The pitfall

The client uses the wrong port, or the server isn't on the port everyone expects: `PORT=8000` in the app while the client uses 8080, a framework's default that changed, or a published port that forwards to the wrong container port (`ports: ["18080:8080"]` for a server on 8000).

## The evidence

| Evidence | What it shows |
|---|---|
| `netns.listeners` of the destination | nothing on the port; listeners reachable from outside the container on other ports (Docker's embedded-DNS listener and loopback-only sockets aside) |
| `probe.tcp` from the source (optional) | refused, or accepted and closed at once through docker-proxy |

## The fix

- Connect to the port it listens on (`api:8000`), or make the server listen on the expected port (check its port setting).
- Through a published port: publish the port it uses (`ports: ["18080:8000"]`).

## False positives and limits

- With more than three other ports, guessing which one is meant is too weak: the finding still says nothing listens on the port, but the fix names no port.
- A destination whose health is still `starting` may open the port later: one level less confident (it has started, so it isn't WR010).
- With replicas behind one name, when another replica serves the port, WR003 is one level less confident and says which ones serve.
- Loopback-only listeners elsewhere don't count: they're unreachable from outside anyway (WR001 mentions them).
- A destination without a healthcheck that started moments ago and listens on nothing at all is WR010's, which supersedes WR003.
- If a connect got through, the listener table is out of date: no finding.
- A connect from the source that **timed out** or found no route, instead of being refused, contradicts a listener finding: something on the path (a host firewall, say) stops the connection too, and fixing the listener may not be enough. The finding is one level less confident and cites the connect as contradicting evidence.
